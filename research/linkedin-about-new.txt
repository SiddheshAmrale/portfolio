I build data and AI platforms that run in production, and I work directly with the people who use them.

At Pittsburgh International Airport I designed and run the One Data Platform end to end, from device and vendor feeds to the AI-enriched data products operations teams use every day:

• Lakehouse: 60 Databricks Lakeflow pipelines and 58 jobs on Unity Catalog across flights, baggage, parking, ground transport, finance, workforce, and customer experience. Auto Loader bronze with schema rescue, SCD2 silver, gold data products with explicit grain.

• AI in the pipeline: LLM enrichment with Databricks ai_query (Llama 3.3 70B) that turns raw FAA NOTAMs and customer feedback into plain-English summaries, with versioned prompts, a model kill switch, and a content-hashed Delta cache so reruns are deterministic and cheap.

• Ingestion: a .NET 8 Azure Functions platform with about 50 vendor and device connectors (Amadeus, SITA, Daifuku, Brock, Vaisala, Workday, TSA, FAA): watermarking, gap detection, time-range reprocessing, and dual writes to ADLS and Azure Data Explorer. 1,300+ unit tests.

• Streaming and IoT: about 70 Azure Functions and a payload decoder for LoRaWAN sensors, people counters, and camera analytics, streaming through IoT Hub and Event Hubs into Kusto for near-real-time occupancy, gate, and parking data.

• Platform: Terraform, Databricks Asset Bundles, Bicep, and Azure DevOps with OIDC and approval-gated prod. Unity Catalog grants reconciled from code. Lineage kept as code.

• In users' hands: Kusto-backed data APIs with freshness alerts, plus React and MSAL apps operations staff use daily (parking kiosks, live gate status map, concessions, staff directory).
